## The five biggest, and what each actually was

### `STRAIGHT_TRIE` — −10.37%

`x` **always turns**. That is the decode band's row floor: a node's row must lie
strictly between its children's, so every branch demands a row of its own — ten
band rows carried nothing but `>]x`.

`SPEC.md` gives `d` as "turn clockwise if BP > 0, **else go straight**", so a node
can *be* its up child's entry. The equivalence is exact iff the up half is a
singleton: after `L−1` shifts BP is precisely the slot's offset in the node's
dyadic interval. All ten gap rows qualified. **Band 32 → 22 rows.**

```
before                        after
>]x                           >]d.rM.........v
   >M...............srWNs1+sv >x >.M..srWNs1+sv
```

It is a **+4.11% regression on its own** — it only pays with `SQUASH_BAND` and
`ROM_TOUCH_DROP` at matched values.

### `HIGH_COLLECTOR` — −6.32%

The dispatch loop was walking **past its own destination**. The vertical term is
`(collector − centre) + |centre − row| + (collector − row)`; for a lane *above*
the root that is `2·collector − 2·row` where Manhattan would be `centre − row` —
the man overshoots the fetch, falls to the bottom of the band, and climbs back
through rows he just fell through. **14 wasted ticks on 69.6% of instructions**,
invisible per-region because the three legs live in three different boxes.

One corridor row above the fetch telescopes all of it away.

### `SEEK_TIGHT_STRUCT_DROPS` — −5.84%

`build_cpu` refused tight drops under the seek drum, justified by *"their entry
geometry has not been proved against a tightened column"* — an admission of
missing evidence, restated as a prohibition. `_tight_struct_entry` never reasoned
about slab bodies at all; it only cares which columns carry risers, and every one
of those facts survives the drum.

What the drum adds is a cost that **cancels**: with `SEEK_TAKEN_DROP_EAST`,
`turn_x == drop_x − 1`, so the drop column drops out of the round trip and `JMPS`
costs the same wherever its entry sits. Structured drops **47,48,49,50 → 18,19,25,36**.

### `FETCH_FOLD` — −3.49%, two columns

The man already walks to the fetch — west along the corridor, or up the riser.
Put the prologue on those cells and he fetches *during* the approach.

```
before          after
v<<<]<          vbr]<
>rbrx           >r.x
.   ]              ]
```

`lane_x0` **12 → 10**. A column is worth **2.436 t/instr**, measured by toggling
`TIGHT_TRIE_COLS`. The corridor and riser converge only at the fetch's own `>`,
so a corridor-only prologue would break the 30.4% that rise the riser —
duplication onto the riser is free, because those were `.` cells already walked.

### `SEEK_TWIN_STATION` — −1.69%

The drum's notice path was **1,079 t/seek of pure travel** while the CPU sat
blocked 927 t/seek waiting for it — latency, not throughput, which is why four
CPU-side seek levers all measured exactly 0.00%.

Both gadgets now turn **north**, so the man cascades *up his own side* instead of
descending to a collector he then walks off. **The collector row, the riser and
column 0 stop existing** (289.7 + 123.0 t/seek → 0).

The request needed no teleport: the drum room has exactly **one** incoming pipe,
so `q`/`r` bind it from anywhere. `S` would have *broken* it — two pipes means two
values, one gadget fires on each, and the drum seeks twice.

## What is genuinely at floor, with the proof

- **The return path.** No ISA glyph relocates a man: `> < ^ v X d a x` set heading
  only, movement is one cell per tick, `H` deletes, `Y` births one cell away.
  `R`/`r` are distance-free but **pipes move values, not men**. 15,756 (home,
  wall) pairs searched on men-v3, **zero legal**.
- **`lane_x0`.** 12 under every lean mode and 200,000 random lean subsets, both
  tiers — necessarily, since `LEAN_TRIE` moves a node *toward* its parent and can
  only shrink the slack the two zero-slack edges lack. (`FETCH_FOLD` then took it
  to 10 by attacking a *different* term: the gap is `1 turn + 3 fetch glyphs +
  5 trie levels + 2 zero-slack penalty`.)
- **The store.** Latency = stages × lap, and both are forced. Every ring is at its
  perimeter floor (a w×h ring has `2(w+h)−4` cells, 4 of them corners that cannot
  work, so k glyphs need `2(w+h)−8 ≥ k`). Fan-out over 910 targets needs two
  levels. Decoder and cell cannot merge because **no glyph reads BP back into A**.
- **The flush's 6-tick read loop**, by parity: `r` does not turn, so the edges in
  and out must be parallel; a closed walk on a 4-connected lattice has even
  length; in a 4-cycle consecutive edges are perpendicular. **No 4-cycle can hold
  a read feeding a test.**
- **Return paths are Manhattan-exact.** Monotone west-then-north, so rerouting one
  is worth 0 by construction. Only ideas that *delete cells* or *shrink `lane_x0`*
  can pay — which is why the shared discard measured exactly 0 (+12 walk, −1
  riser, −11 collector).

row. Condition restated on `mem_x`, not on the pad.

**Moving the eastward routing run up onto `SND`'s row — refused, it is not a free
row.** `SND`'s own glyphs do end at its `v` (x=21) and the run does start east of
them (x=22). But `SND` is the *bottom* lane, and four structured drops fall past
it into the slab band: **JMPS 22, JMPF 23, BRZ 26, BRN 27**. Row 175 carries `.`
at exactly 22, 23, 26 and 27 for that reason. The run's first glyph is a `>` at
**x=22 — JMPS's drop column** — so the merge puts an eastbound turn under a
southbound man on four columns. The eastmost structured drop is 27, so the run
could only begin at 28, which is not where it begins.

**Moving the stream router's touch — worth ~0, and it is not what pins the pad.**
The full touch table for the shipped men-v3 build is `in (9,137)`,
`mem_resp (43,154)`, `mem_req (43,159)`, `rom (7,178)`, `cmd (43,198)`,
`stream_cmd (19,209)`. Exactly one glyph wants `stream_cmd` — `'s' (19,175)`,

## Instrument notes, dearly bought

- **Region heat must use `_region_of`** (smallest enclosing box). The CPU's boxes
  nest, and a naive sum reads **138.83%** of the run. `heat` counts ticks on a
  cell, not executions — a word is `heat − wait` at an `r`.
- **The 3-round screen over-reads a win by 1.39× on average** (spread 0.90–2.35×)
  and reversed sign three times. Ranking instrument, never a measurement.
- **Never price with a cost model that has not been validated per-candidate.** One
  had mean residual 12.9pp and **sign correct 0 of 3** — a candidate modelled at
  −4.5% measured **+14.4%**.
- **Benches that duplicate geometry go stale.** `dispatch_bench`'s lane table
  still listed drops at column 42 after `TUCKED_DROPS` pulled them to 22..32, and
  produced a phantom 17 cells/instr prize. Read geometry from a real build.
- **`check_bindings` must be captured only on the call that survives** —
  `build_for` retries pads and failing trials come through the same hook.
- **Numbers get inherited.** "550 ticks per taken seek" came from dividing the
  seek pool by all 36,145 taken `JMPF`s when only **8,252** are seeks. The real
  figure is 1,755.6, and the bad one framed an entire investigation.

## The corridor, decomposed — and why it is at its floor

`cpu:return:high` (11.66 t/instr) and `cpu:trie` (17.73) are the two largest
regions and neither blocks. Both are the same walk: `drop_x − fetch_x` east
through the trie and along the lane, and the same distance back west along the
corridor. Per-lane, on the real build (`menprobe/corridor.py`, weighted by
measured opcode share):

| | cells | share of the east leg |
|---|---|---|
| `fetch_x` → `lane_x0` (the trie's 7 columns + 2) | **9.000** | 54.6% |
| the lane's own operations | 3.541 | 24.2% |
| `.` padding out to `mem_x` | 2.840 | 19.4% |
| blanks after the last op (drop contention) | 0.275 | 1.9% |
| **mean corridor length** | **15.656** | |

(69.56% of instructions return by the corridor — rows above `hi_row`; the figures
above are weighted over those. Over all lanes the round trip is 30.620
cells/instr and the mean drop column is 24.31.)

The loop is **Manhattan-exact given `drop_x`**: vertical is
`(169−r) + (168−r) + 1 = 2(169−r)`, horizontal is `8 + (drop_x−18) + (drop_x−10)
= 2·drop_x − 20`. So only `drop_x` and `lane_x0` are reducible, and:

- **The lanes are not longer than their micro-programs need.** Only 6 of 22 rows
  have any slack at all between where the trie drops the man and where the lane
  starts, and every one is pinned: `INCM`/`MOVA`/`DIV` are MEM lanes (their `s`
  is at `mem_x` whatever the lane start), `SND`'s is at the DSP column
  `lane_x0+1`, `JMPS`'s drop is a slab entry, and `IN`'s `r` is bind-pinned.
  A per-lane `lane_x0` is worth ≈ 0.
- **`max(prefixes)` is not reducible either**, and not for the reason it looks:
  the pad search sweeps `mem_x` directly (`pads = range(0, 40)`), so
  `lane_x0 + prefixes + pad` spans every column ≥ `lane_x0+1` and the search has
  already rejected everything west of the answer.
- **`cpu:trie` is 7 columns of traverse plus the band depth**, not one thing:
  per-column heat is 5.99/3.74/1.44/2.29/1.91/1.04/1.32. Column 11 carries six
  ticks because it is the root's column and the vertical descent runs down it.
  Roughly 7 is horizontal, ~6.6 is Manhattan vertical, and ~4.1 is the trie's
  zigzag (a node's row need only lie *between* its children's, so a man can
  overshoot and come back). `LEAN_TRIE` is the optimiser for that term and is
  already on, greedy, with a proof (`_lean_row`).

## The ties were ours, not the machine's — worth −0.864%

`check_bindings` refused a §7.1 tie outright, *even one the intended pipe wins*:

```python
if rivals[want] != best or sum(1 for d in rivals.values() if d == best) > 1:
```

`SPEC.md:183` breaks ties by **reading order (top to bottom, left to right)**,
and both engines implement exactly that — `fast_littleman._bind_pipe_ops` picks
`min(candidates, key=(distance, attach_y, attach_x))`, and the reference WASM
`route()` agrees cell for cell. The builder was strictly stronger than the
machine it builds for.

men-v3's `mem_pad` floor was two ties at two rows, and **relieving one exposes
the other**, which is why it looked like a cliff:

| `in_west` | pad-2 blocker | tie? | won by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | `'r' (22,154)` `in` 21 / `mem_resp` 21 | yes | **`in`** — attach (9,137) reads before (43,154) |
| 1..9 | `'r' (22,163)` `rom` 30 / `mem_resp` 30 | yes | **`mem_resp`** — (43,154) reads before (7,178) |

So the first tie is genuinely lost and only moving the I room escapes it; the
second is won and only the relaxed clause admits it. **Neither change is worth
anything alone.** Together: `mem_pad` 3 → 2, `mem_x` 22 → 21, and the 54.16% of
instructions carrying a MEM band lose a column off the lane and another off the
walk back west.

**81,042,708 → 80,342,861, −0.864%**, `passed=True fatal=None`, 496x674
unchanged, taped bit-identical at 140,379,566, `deadman-3d` byte-identical
(`f62d63fd…`/`1bc5e791…`), suite 2855/0. Confirmed three ways: the spec, the
frame gate, and the reference WASM oracle routing `'r' (22,163)` to
`[[44,154],[43,154]]` — `mem_resp`.

The shipped build has **exactly one** binding decided by a tie (that cell). The
next tightest is margin 1 (`'r' (18,153)` wants `in`, `mem_resp` one further),
so this is a one-cell margin and any geometry move can flip it — the failure
mode is a wrong frame, not an exception, and a 3-round screen will not see it.

## Appendix: the ROM two-men experiment, and a correction worth more than it

Proposal: split the ROM between two men so neither walks the full width, skipping
the long `<<<<<<<` wrap.

**Refused — but the first reason given was wrong, and the way it was wrong is the
lesson.** An agent argued the shape is unrepresentable, from `SPEC.md:24` ("At
most one `@` per room") plus `SPEC.md:166` (a pipe's arrowhead sits on **the
source room's** border, so one source room per pipe). Therefore, it said, one pipe
can never have two producers.

**That inference does not hold.** `@` is a *glyph*: line 24 constrains the initial
grid, not the number of men at runtime. `Y` splits a man (`SPEC.md:131`) and both
children live in that room — so two men in the **same** room can both `s` into the
same pipe. One source *room*, two producers. The shape is representable, and the
FIFO-ordering hazard is real rather than moot.

**The refusal survives on economics.** Bucketing the ROM man's heat by the glyph
under him:

| | men-v3 | taped |
|---|---|---|
| standing on `s` (emitting or blocked) | **66.3%** | **84.5%** |
| walking | 33.7% | 15.5% |
| the whole wrap | 2,368 ticks = **0.0260%** | 1,344 = **0.0099%** |

The producer is already **2–5.5× ahead of the consumer**, and *more* so on the
slower tier — the signature of a consumer-bound pipeline. A second ROM man would
simply block longer on a full corridor. The wrap is crossed about once a round,
because the seek drum sends the man up his own ladder instead of round the lap; the
"~300 ticks per read" is ~1–2k ticks *per tour*.

`Y` is separately unusable here: `AGENTS.md` pins that FastLittleman understates
ticks once it is used and the reference engine OOMs on this machine, so there is
**no oracle**. `ROM-RECIRCULATION.md:355` also killed it on grid parity — two
children on one row are always an even number of cells apart.

Recorded as `ROM_TWO_MEN`, empty, with the revisit condition stated numerically:
**re-run the glyph bucket and act only if the `s` share falls below ~50% on
men-v3**, which needs the CPU roughly 2× faster than the 97.14 t/instr this was
measured at.

A per-row trap found on the way: taped's connector row totals 4,048 ticks, but
2,976 of that is the east ladder's `]` drop and the cascades *crossing* it — the
notice path `SEEK_TWIN_STATION` already priced. Read per-row and the wrap looks 3×
its real size.
