## The five biggest, and what each actually was

### `STRAIGHT_TRIE` — −10.37%

`x` **always turns**. That is the decode band's row floor: a node's row must lie
strictly between its children's, so every branch demands a row of its own — ten
band rows carried nothing but `>]x`.

`SPEC.md` gives `d` as "turn clockwise if BP > 0, **else go straight**", so a node
can *be* its up child's entry. The equivalence is exact iff the up half is a
singleton: after `L−1` shifts BP is precisely the slot's offset in the node's
dyadic interval. All ten gap rows qualified. **Band 32 → 22 rows.**

```
before                        after
>]x                           >]d.rM.........v
   >M...............srWNs1+sv >x >.M..srWNs1+sv
```

It is a **+4.11% regression on its own** — it only pays with `SQUASH_BAND` and
`ROM_TOUCH_DROP` at matched values.

### `HIGH_COLLECTOR` — −6.32%

The dispatch loop was walking **past its own destination**. The vertical term is
`(collector − centre) + |centre − row| + (collector − row)`; for a lane *above*
the root that is `2·collector − 2·row` where Manhattan would be `centre − row` —
the man overshoots the fetch, falls to the bottom of the band, and climbs back
through rows he just fell through. **14 wasted ticks on 69.6% of instructions**,
invisible per-region because the three legs live in three different boxes.

One corridor row above the fetch telescopes all of it away.

### `SEEK_TIGHT_STRUCT_DROPS` — −5.84%

`build_cpu` refused tight drops under the seek drum, justified by *"their entry
geometry has not been proved against a tightened column"* — an admission of
missing evidence, restated as a prohibition. `_tight_struct_entry` never reasoned
about slab bodies at all; it only cares which columns carry risers, and every one
of those facts survives the drum.

What the drum adds is a cost that **cancels**: with `SEEK_TAKEN_DROP_EAST`,
`turn_x == drop_x − 1`, so the drop column drops out of the round trip and `JMPS`
costs the same wherever its entry sits. Structured drops **47,48,49,50 → 18,19,25,36**.

### `FETCH_FOLD` — −3.49%, two columns

The man already walks to the fetch — west along the corridor, or up the riser.
Put the prologue on those cells and he fetches *during* the approach.

```
before          after
v<<<]<          vbr]<
>rbrx           >r.x
.   ]              ]
```

`lane_x0` **12 → 10**. A column is worth **2.436 t/instr**, measured by toggling
`TIGHT_TRIE_COLS`. The corridor and riser converge only at the fetch's own `>`,
so a corridor-only prologue would break the 30.4% that rise the riser —
duplication onto the riser is free, because those were `.` cells already walked.

### `SEEK_TWIN_STATION` — −1.69%

The drum's notice path was **1,079 t/seek of pure travel** while the CPU sat
blocked 927 t/seek waiting for it — latency, not throughput, which is why four
CPU-side seek levers all measured exactly 0.00%.

Both gadgets now turn **north**, so the man cascades *up his own side* instead of
descending to a collector he then walks off. **The collector row, the riser and
column 0 stop existing** (289.7 + 123.0 t/seek → 0).

The request needed no teleport: the drum room has exactly **one** incoming pipe,
so `q`/`r` bind it from anywhere. `S` would have *broken* it — two pipes means two
values, one gadget fires on each, and the drum seeks twice.

## What is genuinely at floor, with the proof

- **The return path.** No ISA glyph relocates a man: `> < ^ v X d a x` set heading
  only, movement is one cell per tick, `H` deletes, `Y` births one cell away.
  `R`/`r` are distance-free but **pipes move values, not men**. 15,756 (home,
  wall) pairs searched on men-v3, **zero legal**.
- **`lane_x0`.** 12 under every lean mode and 200,000 random lean subsets, both
  tiers — necessarily, since `LEAN_TRIE` moves a node *toward* its parent and can
  only shrink the slack the two zero-slack edges lack. (`FETCH_FOLD` then took it
  to 10 by attacking a *different* term: the gap is `1 turn + 3 fetch glyphs +
  5 trie levels + 2 zero-slack penalty`.)
- **The store.** Latency = stages × lap, and both are forced. Every ring is at its
  perimeter floor (a w×h ring has `2(w+h)−4` cells, 4 of them corners that cannot
  work, so k glyphs need `2(w+h)−8 ≥ k`). Fan-out over 910 targets needs two
  levels. Decoder and cell cannot merge because **no glyph reads BP back into A**.
- **The flush's 6-tick read loop**, by parity: `r` does not turn, so the edges in
  and out must be parallel; a closed walk on a 4-connected lattice has even
  length; in a 4-cycle consecutive edges are perpendicular. **No 4-cycle can hold
  a read feeding a test.**
- **Return paths are Manhattan-exact.** Monotone west-then-north, so rerouting one
  is worth 0 by construction. Only ideas that *delete cells* or *shrink `lane_x0`*
  can pay — which is why the shared discard measured exactly 0 (+12 walk, −1
  riser, −11 collector).

## The corridor, decomposed — and why it is at its floor

`cpu:return:high` (11.66 t/instr) and `cpu:trie` (17.73) are the two largest
regions and neither blocks. Both are the same walk: `drop_x − fetch_x` east
through the trie and along the lane, and the same distance back west along the
corridor. Per-lane, on the real build (`menprobe/corridor.py`, weighted by
measured opcode share):

| | cells | share of the east leg |
|---|---|---|
| `fetch_x` → `lane_x0` (the trie's 7 columns + 2) | **9.000** | 54.6% |
| the lane's own operations | 3.541 | 24.2% |
| `.` padding out to `mem_x` | 2.840 | 19.4% |
| blanks after the last op (drop contention) | 0.275 | 1.9% |
| **mean corridor length** | **15.656** | |

(69.56% of instructions return by the corridor — rows above `hi_row`; the figures
above are weighted over those. Over all lanes the round trip is 30.620
cells/instr and the mean drop column is 24.31.)

The loop is **Manhattan-exact given `drop_x`**: vertical is
`(169−r) + (168−r) + 1 = 2(169−r)`, horizontal is `8 + (drop_x−18) + (drop_x−10)
= 2·drop_x − 20`. So only `drop_x` and `lane_x0` are reducible, and:

- **The lanes are not longer than their micro-programs need.** Only 6 of 22 rows
  have any slack at all between where the trie drops the man and where the lane
  starts, and every one is pinned: `INCM`/`MOVA`/`DIV` are MEM lanes (their `s`
  is at `mem_x` whatever the lane start), `SND`'s is at the DSP column
  `lane_x0+1`, `JMPS`'s drop is a slab entry, and `IN`'s `r` is bind-pinned.
  A per-lane `lane_x0` is worth ≈ 0.
- **`max(prefixes)` is not reducible either**, and not for the reason it looks:
  the pad search sweeps `mem_x` directly (`pads = range(0, 40)`), so
  `lane_x0 + prefixes + pad` spans every column ≥ `lane_x0+1` and the search has
  already rejected everything west of the answer.
- **`cpu:trie` is 7 columns of traverse plus the band depth**, not one thing:
  per-column heat is 5.99/3.74/1.44/2.29/1.91/1.04/1.32. Column 11 carries six
  ticks because it is the root's column and the vertical descent runs down it.
  Roughly 7 is horizontal, ~6.6 is Manhattan vertical, and ~4.1 is the trie's
  zigzag (a node's row need only lie *between* its children's, so a man can
  overshoot and come back). `LEAN_TRIE` is the optimiser for that term and is
  already on, greedy, with a proof (`_lean_row`).

## The ties were ours, not the machine's — worth −0.864%

`check_bindings` refused a §7.1 tie outright, *even one the intended pipe wins*:

```python
if rivals[want] != best or sum(1 for d in rivals.values() if d == best) > 1:
```

`SPEC.md:183` breaks ties by **reading order (top to bottom, left to right)**,
and both engines implement exactly that — `fast_littleman._bind_pipe_ops` picks
`min(candidates, key=(distance, attach_y, attach_x))`, and the reference WASM
`route()` agrees cell for cell. The builder was strictly stronger than the
machine it builds for.

men-v3's `mem_pad` floor was two ties at two rows, and **relieving one exposes
the other**, which is why it looked like a cliff:

| `in_west` | pad-2 blocker | tie? | won by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | `'r' (22,154)` `in` 21 / `mem_resp` 21 | yes | **`in`** — attach (9,137) reads before (43,154) |
| 1..9 | `'r' (22,163)` `rom` 30 / `mem_resp` 30 | yes | **`mem_resp`** — (43,154) reads before (7,178) |

So the first tie is genuinely lost and only moving the I room escapes it; the
second is won and only the relaxed clause admits it. **Neither change is worth
anything alone.** Together: `mem_pad` 3 → 2, `mem_x` 22 → 21, and the 54.16% of
instructions carrying a MEM band lose a column off the lane and another off the

**81,042,708 → 80,342,861, −0.864%**, `passed=True fatal=None`, 496x674

## Appendix: the ROM two-men experiment, and a correction worth more than it

Proposal: split the ROM between two men so neither walks the full width, skipping
the long `<<<<<<<` wrap.

**Refused — but the first reason given was wrong, and the way it was wrong is the
lesson.** An agent argued the shape is unrepresentable, from `SPEC.md:24` ("At
most one `@` per room") plus `SPEC.md:166` (a pipe's arrowhead sits on **the
source room's** border, so one source room per pipe). Therefore, it said, one pipe
can never have two producers.

**That inference does not hold.** `@` is a *glyph*: line 24 constrains the initial
grid, not the number of men at runtime. `Y` splits a man (`SPEC.md:131`) and both
children live in that room — so two men in the **same** room can both `s` into the
same pipe. One source *room*, two producers. The shape is representable, and the
FIFO-ordering hazard is real rather than moot.

**The refusal survives on economics.** Bucketing the ROM man's heat by the glyph
under him:

| | men-v3 | taped |
|---|---|---|
| standing on `s` (emitting or blocked) | **66.3%** | **84.5%** |
| walking | 33.7% | 15.5% |
| the whole wrap | 2,368 ticks = **0.0260%** | 1,344 = **0.0099%** |

The producer is already **2–5.5× ahead of the consumer**, and *more* so on the
slower tier — the signature of a consumer-bound pipeline. A second ROM man would
simply block longer on a full corridor. The wrap is crossed about once a round,
because the seek drum sends the man up his own ladder instead of round the lap; the
"~300 ticks per read" is ~1–2k ticks *per tour*.

`Y` is separately unusable here: `AGENTS.md` pins that FastLittleman understates
ticks once it is used and the reference engine OOMs on this machine, so there is
**no oracle**. `ROM-RECIRCULATION.md:355` also killed it on grid parity — two
children on one row are always an even number of cells apart.

Recorded as `ROM_TWO_MEN`, empty, with the revisit condition stated numerically:
**re-run the glyph bucket and act only if the `s` share falls below ~50% on
men-v3**, which needs the CPU roughly 2× faster than the 97.14 t/instr this was
measured at.

A per-row trap found on the way: taped's connector row totals 4,048 ticks, but
2,976 of that is the east ladder's `]` drop and the cascades *crossing* it — the
notice path `SEEK_TWIN_STATION` already priced. Read per-row and the wrap looks 3×
its real size.

## Putting §7.1 in a solver, and what it immediately found

Every pad floor in this document was found the same way: sweep one knob, watch
`check_bindings` refuse, record the number. That method cannot find a floor that
only moves when *two* knobs move together, and it never did.

§7.1 is a constraint, so it belongs in a constraint solver. Glyph positions
fixed, every pipe attachment `(tx,ty)` an integer variable, and for each glyph
with intended pipe `w` against every rival `q` in the same direction pool:

```
lex_lt( (d_w, ty_w, tx_w), (d_q, ty_q, tx_q) )      d_p = |tx_p − gx| + |ty_p − gy|
```

Strict lex-less, because `w` must *win* the tie rather than merely reach it —
the engines' key verbatim. 63 glyphs × ~3 rivals ≈ 200 constraints; milliseconds.
`scratch/deadman3d-opt/z3/z3bind.py`.

**Validated before it was trusted.** The model reproduces, on men-v3:

| touches pinned | pad 0 | pad 1 | pad 2 | pad 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `in_west` 9 | unsat | unsat | **sat** | sat |
| `in_west` default | unsat | unsat | unsat | **sat** |

and finds exactly one tie-decided binding on the shipped grid (`'r' (22,163)`,
`rom` 30 vs `mem_resp` 30). That is the whole of the previous section, re-derived
from the rule rather than from forty builds.

**Then the question sweeps cannot ask.** Relax every attachment to anywhere on
the 496×674 grid:

```
pad 0: sat      pad 1: sat      pad 2: sat
```

So the pad was never a *binding* floor — it was a floor on the touch positions we
happened to have. Freed one at a time, only two pipes can unlock pad 1:

```
mem_resp   (43,154) -> (39,163)   sat
rom         (7,178) -> (14,188)   sat
cmd / in / mem_req / stream_cmd   unsat
```

`ROM_TOUCH_DROP` is the knob that moves `rom` — in y only, x pinned at 7 — and
the constrained question (`tx_rom == 7`, y free) is SAT as well. Checked against
**real captured geometry** rather than the model: drops 5..10 unsat at pad 1,
**11..17 sat**. 21 rounds, `passed=True`, box unchanged: **80,342,861 →
79,341,770, −1.246%.**

The knob was already there. Eight one-dimensional sweeps this session never
varied it *against* the pad, so it was never seen.

### What the solver does and does not decide

- The model holds glyphs fixed while touches move; real knobs often move both
  (`ROM_TOUCH_DROP` also sets `fetch_y`). A SAT answer is a **candidate**, and
  only a captured build is evidence. The `(14,188)` witness was unreachable; the
  useful answer came from asking the *constrained* question.
- **Feasible is not faster.** Binding is a constraint, not the objective — the
  drop buys a pad column at 2.436 t/instr and spends two rows of ROM corridor,
  and it happened to win. `SQUASH_BAND` +2 to hold the corridor difference
  constant does not build: the store's request wall lands on row 159 against the
  adapter's row 157, and a straight leg needs them level.
- **UNSAT under relaxation would have been a proof** — no repositioning of any
  pipe, legal or not, could beat it. It came back SAT, which is why there was
  something to find.
- The ~27 t/instr the CPU spends **blocked** on the drum and store is emergent
  from concurrent simulation, not a constraint over coordinates. Nothing here
  touches it.

**taped re-derived and unchanged at pad 2.** Its recorded reason — "a tie, which
`check_bindings` fails as hard as a loss" — is now stale, but the floor survives
on the other half: pad 1 loses `'r' (21,148)` and pad 0 loses `'r' (20,148)`,
both to `in`, at drops 16/18/20 alike. `rom` is not the rival there, so sliding
it does nothing, and `INPUT_NORTH_WEST` already has the I room at `CX + 1`.

### The frontier is a line, not a cliff — pad 0

`mem_pad` and `ROM_TOUCH_DROP` trade at an exact rate: **two drop rows buy one
pad column.** A pad column moves the band's westmost `r` one cell *nearer* `rom`
and one *further* from `mem_resp`, so the drop gives back two rows to restore the
margin. That is why every one-knob sweep hit a wall — the wall only moves when
both move.

| pad | `mem_x` | binding window | min `eff` |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 13 | drop ≥ 9 | −1 |
| 1 | 12 | drop ≥ 11 | −1 |

## The ceiling, and why the CPU stopped being the question

| | t/instr | share |
|---|---|---|
| store blocking | 83.71 | 55.1% |
| everything else | 68.21 | 44.9% |

Measured read latency decomposes as **101.0 fixed pipeline floor + 66.2 chain
traversal (23.0 t/hop × 2.879 hops) + 59.7 ring rotation and read-after-write**
= 226.9 mean. The chain hop was measured three independent ways and the
pipe-travel term agrees with prediction to 0.34%.

At 0.369 reads/instr the 101-tick floor is **37.3 t/instr that no routing
reaches**, so the best conceivable taped store lands at **×1.44** and 2× would
need a mean read latency of 21 ticks. **2× is arithmetically unreachable on the
drum**, and three independent instruments agree the CPU is done:

- liveness prover: **0.045%** of CPU walking removable in total (63,240 ticks)
- exact row-assignment solve: **0.66%** in ticks from the combinatorial optimum
- direct test: moving the JMPS drop column 2 and 4 columns west is **identical to
  the tick** — the eastward run pays back exactly what the drop saves

### The one lever that would move the ceiling

**28.6% of store traffic — 69,266 write→single-read episodes, 138,532 accesses —
is pure scratch**: a word stored only to survive an ACC clobber, then read once.
`A` is clobbered by every fetch and `BP` is branch-only, so any two-live-value
expression must spill. `Q`/`TMP`/`DAC` are not variables, they are a register
file implemented on tape.

`PUSH`/`POP` exist as opcodes **22/23** and `machine.py` builds **no SPILL
block**. A spill ring converts each episode from two tape round trips into two
short hops — estimated **−15% to −26%**, and it is the only candidate that moves
the 101-tick floor itself, because that floor only binds accesses reaching the
tape.

## The SPILL block: the refusal was fake, the premise was wrong

`PUSH`/`POP` exist as opcodes 22/23 with a real `SpillRing` in `lm1/store.py`,
emulator handlers, and tests pinning LIFO order and the high-water mark. The
*only* thing missing was a `SEM_MICRO` entry, so `plan()` raised **"no hardware
micro-program for ['PUSH','POP']"**. No guard, no floor, no failing check.

The recorded justification — `machine.py:267` "indexed memory: `LDP`/`STP`, and
no SPILL block", plus `test_indexed_memory_needs_no_spill_block` — argues exactly
one thing: the sign-biased STORE request removed the literal that used to clobber
`A`, so **`LDP`/`STP`** need no pointer parking. True, and silent on a program
that wants scratch slots. It says *no demand*, not *no possibility*. Another
conditional recorded as an absolute.

**But my estimate was wrong by six times, and the error was in the premise.** I
briefed −15% to −26% from 28.6% of store traffic being scratch. Measured:

| | episodes | % of store traffic |
|---|---:|---:|
| write→exactly-one-read | 72,140 | 32.53% |
| plain `ST`→plain `LD` | 57,632 | 25.99% |
| **statically convertible** | 7,684 | **3.46%** |
| **+ LIFO-clean on the real trace** | **7,588** | **3.42%** |

The collapse is **not nesting** — it is *static purity*. A rewrite is per source
line: every dynamic execution of an `ST` site must be scratch, and every reader
must be a converted `POP`. Closing that bipartite relation leaves 60 `ST` and 59
`LD` sites. Realistic gain **−2% to −4%**.

Nesting is a real problem too, just not the binding one: over all episodes
**58.0% violate LIFO at max depth 21**. On the convertible subset a stack is
right — 0.6% violations at depth 3, and dropping 14 sites gives LIFO-clean at
depth 2.

**The rewrite validates bit-exactly**: 60 `ST`→`PUSH`, 59 `LD`→`POP`, identical
instruction count, **identical `wall_writes` sha** (`bf1fd400`), spill high-water
2, ROM 9,217 → 9,098 words, 15,176 accesses off the tape.

**The block binds; it was not drawn.** Sweeping all four CPU walls with the
verbatim `check_bindings` key found **100 joint solutions** where all 65 glyphs
bind — but the window is one-dimensional: `req_row` is pinned to a *single* row
at every column, because the memory `r`/`s` glyphs attach on the same east wall,
so a pipe near enough for a spill lane is near enough to steal a memory lane.
With `|req − resp| ≤ 8` a 6-tall room cannot stand between the attaches, and the
store structure occupies rows 174–179. A three-column routing would fit.

So: **feasible, ~3.4% of traffic, −2% to −4%.** That still clears the ×1.44
ceiling — 150.86 → ~145–148 against a 149.74 bound — because these accesses stop
reaching the tape at all. But it is not the lever that reaches 2×, and nothing
else is either.

## The store was synthesised from scratch, and the shipped one won

Everything in this file is incremental — shave the chain of 11 gates feeding
11 ring workers. So the framework was pointed at the other question: given
the operations, the pipe endpoints and the traffic, and **none of the
existing rooms**, what would you build?

Answer: **the same thing.** Nothing measured below the control (79,223,703).
Best synthesised block **+1.493%**, and it cost +254 columns of machine.

### The transport theorem, which is why

Two coefficients measured before anything was scored:

| instrument | measurement | derived |
|---|---|---|
| `store_chain_pad` 0/4/8 | +2,296,028 / +4,594,436 t, dead linear | **574,100 t per link cell** → ÷ 597,856 read link-crossings = **0.960 t/crossing/cell** |
| chain-order perturbation | +782,692 t on +52,088 crossings | **15.03 t per read link-crossing** |

So: **a pipe cell is exactly one tick of read latency, unconditionally. A
room crossed by `R` costs zero for its width, because `R` has no distance
term.** Therefore the cheapest way to move a request *D* columns east is a
chain of rooms, one per bank pitch, at ~11.7–15 t each — **which is exactly
what the gate chain is.**

Every alternative topology fails at the same place: its fan-out puts two
destinations more than one pitch apart and pays the difference in pipe
cells.

| topology | modelled | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Huffman tree over 11 banks | −1.02% before pipes | distribution is near-geometric (entropy 2.575 bits), so the chain is already a near-optimal unary code; siblings ≥48 columns apart eat the 1% many times over |
| broadcast-and-filter, one room 11 sends | fan-out averages **89.1 t/read** vs the chain's 21.7 | **+27.4% of run** |
| two-sided interior-peeling gate | −0.51% of crossings, +2 test cells on every traversal | **net +1.8%** |
| multi-way gate, 3 local pipes | sub-banks 48/96 columns away | dead |
| **forked adapter → two chains** | crossings/read 1.055 vs 1.856 → **−2.6% net** | **the only survivor, not built** — needs a new forking adapter, a second gate strip, and a block-height change feeding back through `mem_out_row` |

**The chain is not a legacy shape. It is the optimal long-haul transport on
this machine, which happens to do the decode for free on the way.**

### 11 banks is measured, not inherited

9, 10, 12, 13, 14 and 15 all lose after a DP cut, a simulated anneal on the
boundaries, an exact end-peeling order DP and a re-derived rotation set.
Splitting the hottest bank **+2.74%**; merging the two coldest **+2.61%**;
all-batch-2 **+2.29%**. And the shipped end-peeling order is within **0.106
hops** of the unconstrained optimum, so unlocking arbitrary permutations
buys almost nothing.

### The part worth admitting

**A fitted synthesiser seeded from the shipped cut converges back to it.**
The shipped cut carries ~1.8M ticks (**2.3%**) of advantage that no feature
countable off the trace explains. A session of incremental tuning found
something the synthesiser cannot see, and its failure to beat it *is* the
result.

Also recorded: **`f_same` cannot carry the weight this file puts on it.**
The two rotation experiments imply contradictory coefficients (0.95 vs 16.2
t/rotation), and a 6-feature fit over 12 measured builds is unidentifiable —
ring coefficients of 158 t/rotation, a *negative* queueing coefficient, RMS

## `Y` — where the split glyph pays, and where it provably cannot

`Y` births two children with identical A, B and BP, the parent dies, and `H`
halts a spare — so a fork costs **one tick** and disposal is free. The question
is never whether it works; it is whether the second man has anything to do.

### The rule

**`Y` pays only where the children need no ordering with respect to the
parent.** Everything measured on this machine follows from that one line.

| site | verdict | why |
|---|---|---|
| adapter (`_ADAPTER_FORK`, shipped once) | **-2 ticks** on the read leg | two men each carried their own copy of one word; neither had to agree with the other. Superseded by `v4`, which re-encodes to *one* word — at which point there is no second job |
| `SND` | **exactly 0.000%** | measured by padding its lane **+16 cells** for an identical tick. Its walk is entirely inside a gap |
| `ST` | **~1.2% by slope, unbuildable today** | 0.24-0.30%/cell over 20 cells, dead linear to +16. Blocked geometrically: `HIGH_COLLECTOR` is the row above and `LOW_COLLECTOR` the row below, so both children land on a return corridor and the arm has nowhere to stand |
| drain / rotator | **provably cannot win** | see below |

### Why a fork drain cannot beat the ladder

Three independent facts, any one of them fatal:

1. **The pipe is the bound, not the man.** A destination cell offers one value
   per tick, so `n` discards cost `>= n` ticks with one man or a thousand.
   `drain.py`'s ladder is already `n + 5*bits` — one read per tick plus a
   constant — so only the constant was ever available.
2. **The parent may not leave early.** The drain exists so the man does not
   reach the target instruction until the preceding words are gone. Children
   draining asynchronously means the CPU fetches a word nobody consumed: a
   wrong frame, not an error.
3. **The parent wins every contested read anyway.** Runners act in creation
   order within a tick and `Y`'s right child *keeps the parent's slot*, so a
   parent racing its own children always takes the value. They starve and the
   fork degenerates to the serial walk.

Gating the parent on the children needs a synchronisation pipe, which
re-serialises exactly what the fork removed. An earlier estimate of 2-3.5% for
this is **withdrawn**; it priced the constant without checking the ordering.

### The corollary worth keeping

A fork hides a *walk* only when the walk was on the critical path, and this
machine has been tuned against that for a year: post-send walking is 0.019%/tick
against pre-send's 0.27%. So the sites where `Y` helps are the ones where a
single man is **holding two values at once** (the adapter's `M`/`W` shuffle) or
**paying a constant to skip work it will not do** — not the ones where he is
walking. Every place tested against a walk, the walk was already free.
